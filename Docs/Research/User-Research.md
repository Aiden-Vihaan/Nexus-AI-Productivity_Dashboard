NEXUS — User Research Framework

1. Research Objective

The purpose of this research is to understand the cognitive and behavioral difficulties people experience when planning and executing knowledge work.

The research will focus on:

- prioritization,
- planning,
- workload estimation,
- task switching,
- procrastination,
- deadline pressure,
- scheduling,
- interruptions,
- productivity reflection,
- and trust in AI recommendations.

---

2. Primary Research Questions

Planning

- How do users decide what to work on?
- What information do they consider when prioritizing?
- How often do plans change during the day?
- What causes plans to fail?

Workload

- How do users determine whether a workload is realistic?
- How accurately do they estimate task duration?
- What happens when they underestimate work?

Execution

- What causes users to postpone tasks?
- How frequently do users switch between tasks?
- What interrupts focused work?

Reflection

- Do users understand why they were productive or unproductive?
- Which productivity metrics are actually useful?
- How do users evaluate whether a week went well?

Artificial Intelligence

- What productivity decisions would users trust AI to assist with?
- What decisions require human confirmation?
- What information would users want to see before accepting a recommendation?
- What would make an AI recommendation feel trustworthy?

---

3. User Research Themes

The research will investigate five themes:

Cognitive Load

The mental effort required to continuously decide what to do next.

Planning Friction

The effort required to construct and maintain realistic plans.

Context Loss

The difficulty of making good decisions when relevant information is distributed across tasks, calendars, projects, and notes.

Behavioral Drift

The difference between intended plans and actual behavior.

AI Trust

The relationship between automation, transparency, explainability, and user control.

---

4. Initial User Hypotheses

These are hypotheses to validate through research.

H1

Users frequently create plans that become unrealistic as the day progresses.

H2

Users often prioritize tasks using simple heuristics such as deadline or perceived urgency.

H3

Users have limited visibility into the behavioral reasons behind recurring productivity problems.

H4

Users may accept AI recommendations more readily when the system explains its reasoning.

H5

Users prefer AI assistance that preserves control over important decisions.

H6

Users experience cognitive overload when productivity systems expose too much information without clear prioritization.

---

5. Research Methods

The research program may include:

Competitive Analysis

Study existing productivity products.

Semi-Structured Interviews

Interview people who regularly manage multiple concurrent responsibilities.

Diary Study

Ask participants to record planning decisions, interruptions, and rescheduling behavior over several days.

Usability Testing

Evaluate early NEXUS prototypes.

Prototype Comparison

Compare conventional prioritization against context-aware recommendations.

Quantitative Evaluation

Measure recommendation acceptance, completion rates, planning time, and perceived workload.

---

6. Research Ethics

Research should avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive personal information.

Participants should understand:

- what information is collected,
- why it is collected,
- how it is stored,
- and how it will be used.

Participation should be voluntary.

The NEXUS research process should prioritize privacy and informed consent.

---

7. Research Output

Research findings will eventually be synthesized into:

- personas,
- user journeys,
- pain points,
- opportunity areas,
- design principles,
- product requirements,
- and evaluation criteria.

The initial hypotheses must remain explicitly labeled as hypotheses until supported or rejected by evidence.
