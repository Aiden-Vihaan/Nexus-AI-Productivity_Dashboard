# NEXUS — Scheduling Intelligence

**Version:** 0.1
**Status:** Product Architecture

---

## 1. Purpose

The NEXUS Scheduling Intelligence system determines suitable time windows for work based on task requirements, user availability, deadlines, dependencies, workload, and contextual constraints.

The objective is not to maximize calendar utilization.

The objective is to create a realistic and sustainable plan.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> NEXUS schedules work around human capacity rather than treating available time as an unlimited resource.

---

## 3. Scheduling Inputs

The scheduling engine considers:

- task duration,
- task priority,
- impact,
- urgency,
- deadline,
- cognitive load,
- energy requirement,
- dependencies,
- calendar commitments,
- available capacity,
- preferred working periods,
- historical completion behavior.

---

## 4. Scheduling Outputs

The engine may produce:

- recommended start time,
- recommended end time,
- alternative time windows,
- conflict warnings,
- deadline risk,
- schedule confidence,
- rescheduling recommendations.

---

## 5. Scheduling Philosophy

NEXUS should avoid filling every available minute.

A healthy schedule should preserve capacity for:

- interruptions,
- unexpected work,
- transitions,
- recovery,
- and uncertainty.

---

## 6. Human Control

The scheduling engine recommends plans.

Users retain control over:

- accepting recommendations,
- modifying schedules,
- locking calendar events,
- moving tasks,
- rejecting recommendations.

---

## 7. Explainability

Every significant scheduling recommendation should be explainable through observable factors.

Example:

> Recommended for 10:30 because you have a 90-minute uninterrupted window, the task requires high cognitive focus, and the deadline is approaching.

---

## 8. Adaptation

The schedule should be recalculated when important context changes.

Examples:

- task completed early,
- task takes longer than expected,
- meeting added,
- deadline changed,
- task becomes blocked,
- new high-priority work appears.

---

## 9. Scheduling Principle

A good schedule is not the schedule containing the most work.

A good schedule is the schedule most likely to survive reality.
